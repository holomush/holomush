<objective>
Close the phase's row-level identity model: give the ABAC stack the attributes a
`viewer:` subject needs in order to satisfy the **same** `visibility` /
`visible_to` / `excluded_from` row semantics the shipped character-flavored
`seed:property-*` family evaluates, and wire every provider into the production
stack.

Purpose — **this plan exists because of cross-AI review HIGH #10/#11
(02-REVIEWS.md), which is the sharpest finding in the set.** The earlier plan had
the viewer twins comparing `principal.viewer.player_id` against row data that is
**character**-keyed. `seed:property-private-read` compares
`resource.property.owner == principal.character.id`
(`internal/access/policy/seed.go:120`), and `visible_to`/`excluded_from` are lists
of character ids (`internal/access/policy/seed.go:136,142`). A player id compared
against a character id never matches. Because a missing or non-matching key
evaluates **false** (`internal/access/policy/dsl/evaluator.go:216`), every
`private`, `restricted` and `admin` profile field would become permanently
invisible — no error, no failing test, fail-closed. The visible symptom is "the
profile looks bare", which §8.5.1.1 records as exactly the symptom that provokes
the forbidden repair of dropping term B.

**Why derived player-keyed peers, and not a viewer character-id list.** The
obvious fix — put the viewer's character ids in the subject bag — is only half
expressible in the shipped DSL, and the half it cannot express is the half that
matters. `X in Y` (`evalInExpr`) requires a **scalar** left operand and a slice
right operand, so `resource.property.owner in principal.viewer.character_ids`
would work for ownership; but `visible_to`/`excluded_from` need
"any of my characters is in this list", which is a list-to-list intersection.
`containsAll`/`containsAny` take a **literal** needle list
(`ContainsCondition.List`), not an attribute reference, so they cannot express it
either. There is no DSL expression that intersects two attribute lists. The
alternatives were: change the DSL grammar of a security-critical evaluator, or
resolve the character→player relation **server-side, in the provider, where every
other cross-entity resolution in this file already happens**. This plan takes the
second, following `ParentLocationResolver`
(`internal/access/policy/attribute/property.go:17-21,29-34`) as the precedent.

That is **not** the "substitute player_id for character IDs" the review warns
against. Nothing compares a player id to a character id. The provider resolves the
row's character-keyed fields into player-keyed peers once, server-side, and the
policy compares player to player.

Output: `PlayerRoles` behind a func-field seam, three derived `property.*` peers,
a character-owner resolver, and every provider registered in `BuildABACStack`.

**Union semantics, stated as a decision.** A player is in `visible_to_players` iff
ANY of that player's characters is in `visible_to`. This matches the shipped
`PlayerHasRole` any-character-of-the-player semantics that §10.5 makes normative,
and it is the same union `PlayerRoles` returns. For `excluded_from_players` the
union means a player is excluded if **any** of their characters is excluded — the
fail-closed direction for a `forbid`, and deliberately so.
</objective>

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| `entity_properties` row → policy attribute bag | Character-keyed row fields become the data a player-flavored subject is evaluated against. A wrong derivation here decides every profile read. |
| `character_roles` storage → player-scoped role read | A character-scoped table read with player-scoped semantics; the blast radius of a role is the player, not the character. |
| `ABACConfig` → `BuildABACStack` | An unpopulated seam does not error; it silently removes a whole attribute namespace. |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-02-82 | Information disclosure | viewer twins keyed on character-keyed row data | high | mitigate | A `viewer:` subject carries a player id; `owner`, `visible_to` and `excluded_from` carry character ids. Comparing them never matches, and a non-matching key is FALSE, so every private, restricted and admin field goes permanently invisible with no error — and the visible symptom provokes §8.5.1.1's forbidden repair. Closed by deriving player-keyed peers from the row itself and comparing player to player. |
| T-02-83 | Elevation of privilege | derived peer computed from the caller | high | mitigate | A peer attribute populated from the requesting subject rather than from the row would make every row match its own reader — a total authorization bypass that reads as a working feature. Closed by deriving exclusively from `prop.Owner` / `prop.VisibleTo` / `prop.ExcludedFrom`, by a comment fixing that at the emission site, and by a test asserting the emitted `owner_player_id` for a row owned by someone else is NOT the caller's player id. |
| T-02-84 | Information disclosure | partial derived-peer set on resolver failure | high | mitigate | Emitting some derived peers and not others makes a verdict depend on which query failed: a `restricted` twin could permit while the `private` twin denies during the same outage. Closed by omitting all three together, with all three witnesses false, asserted directly. |
| T-02-85 | Elevation of privilege | unresolvable owner read as ownerless | high | mitigate | An owner whose character row is missing must not collapse into "no owner" — an ownerless row is a DIFFERENT authorization case. Closed by omitting `owner_player_id` with `has_owner_player_id=false` while leaving the character-keyed `owner` and its own witness untouched, so the two cases stay distinguishable to a policy. |
| T-02-86 | Elevation of privilege | `excluded_from_players` union direction | medium | accept | Union semantics mean a player is excluded if ANY of their characters is excluded. For a `forbid` that is the fail-closed direction, and it matches the `PlayerHasRole` union §10.5 makes normative. Recorded as a decision so a later reader does not "fix" it into an all-characters rule that would let a player evade a `forbid` by reading with a different character. |
| T-02-87 | Elevation of privilege | unpopulated `ABACConfig` seam | high | mitigate | A nil `PlayerRoleLookup` or `CharacterOwnerResolver` removes a whole attribute namespace without erroring, default-denying entire policy families invisibly. Closed by populating both at the production composition root, documenting the nil consequence on each field, and asserting resolution end-to-end through the stack `BuildABACStack` returns. |
| T-02-88 | Elevation of privilege | emitted-but-undeclared attribute | medium | mitigate | The resolver drops and counts any key not in the namespace schema, so an emitted-but-undeclared `owner_player_id` is silently absent — the same invisible fail-closed as never emitting it. Closed by the emitted-vs-declared symmetric-difference test, demonstrated RED. |
