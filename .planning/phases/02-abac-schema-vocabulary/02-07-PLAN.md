<objective>
Append every new seed policy this phase ships to `internal/access/policy/seed.go`
in one change: the three tier-floor policies, profile reachability, the five
viewer-flavored read twins, the admin-section gate, and the two `seed:profile-public-read`
entries that widen off-location character reads.

This plan is the single owner of `internal/access/policy/seed.go` for Phase 2.
Plans `02-08`, `02-09` and `02-10` consume these policies and prove their
behaviour; none of them edits this file, so no two plans race on it.

Purpose: seed policies are Go struct literals installed by `policy.Bootstrap` at
boot, so landing them together makes the whole family reviewable as one artifact —
which matters here, because §8.5.1's conjunction is only safe if the tier-floor
family and the row-keyed family stay in **separate evaluations**, and that property
is visible only when both are read side by side.
Output: eleven new entries in `SeedPolicies()` and the `viewerProvider` test double
that lets later plans assert them.

**Open Question 1 settled here.** RESEARCH asked whether `seed:profile-public-read`
is one policy or two. PROFILE-11's scope covers **both** public `entity_properties`
rows and `characters.description`, which are two different ABAC resources
(`property:` and `character:`) gated today by two different colocation-conditioned
seeds. This plan ships **two** entries under one conceptual name —
`seed:profile-public-read-property` and `seed:profile-public-read-character`. The
single-policy alternative would need a bare `resource` clause, which also matches
`location:`, `object:` and `stream:`; RESEARCH names that the most dangerous of the
three options and this plan rejects it explicitly. The deviation from PROFILE-11's
"one new seed policy" wording is a naming choice, not a scope change, and it is
routed to `abac-reviewer` with D-05's §8.5.1.1 amendment.

**D-02 is implemented, not relitigated.** §8.5.1.1's option 2 — term B evaluating
against a co-located character subject and DENYING where one does not exist — is
REJECTED. An anonymous viewer has no character, `profile.pronouns` is an
`entity_properties` row seeded at the `anonymous` floor, so term B would deny it and
a reachable profile would carry `name` without `pronouns` — a §8.8 / INV-PRIVACY-10
violation that fails **closed** and therefore no test in the suite would catch. Term
B is issued against viewer-flavored read policies only.

**Three tier-floor policies, one per rung**, named

**The action token `read_profile_attribute` is the term-A/term-B separator, and it
is pinned here rather than left to the consuming plan.** §8.5.1 requires two
separate evaluations ANDed by the caller; if both evaluations carried the action
`read` against the same `property:<id>` resource, each would match BOTH families
and the conjunction would silently reduce to the additive shape §8.5.1.1 exists to
prevent. Giving the tier-floor family its own action makes term A match only the
tier floors and term B (action `read`) match only the row-keyed family. Actions are
free-form strings in this engine and the repo already carries domain-specific
tokens — `list_character_directory` and `read_unrestricted_history` are the in-tree
precedent — so this is house style, not a new mechanism. Record the token in a
comment on the block, naming what it separates and why, because a future reader
seeing two actions on one resource will otherwise be tempted to unify them.

Transcribe the clearing tests verbatim:
`principal.viewer.tier in ["anonymous", "guest", "player"]` for the anonymous floor,
`principal.viewer.tier in ["guest", "player"]` for the guest floor, and
`principal.viewer.tier in ["player"]` for the player floor. Never `>=`, never `>`,
never a numeric rank attribute — the three v0.13 tokens sort in ladder order by
alphabetical accident, and each of `spectator`, `unverified` and `visitor` sorts
lexicographically above `player`, so a `>=` test would hand a newly appended rung
the highest clearance in the system on the day the token is added, with no policy
edit anywhere.
