<objective>
Append every new seed policy this phase ships to `internal/access/policy/seed.go`
in one change: the tier-floor policies, profile reachability, the five
viewer-flavored read twins, the admin-section gate, and the ONE
`seed:profile-public-read-property` entry that widens off-location public property
reads — **ten** entries in total.

The count was eleven until cross-AI review cycle 3. **D-29 removes
`seed:profile-public-read-character`** — the unconditional
`permit(principal is character, action in ["read"], resource is character)` — from
this phase entirely; it gates a projection returning `PlayerId` and `LocationId` to
every character including every ephemeral guest, and it moves to Phase 4 to land with
the narrowing that makes it safe. Task 3's action carries the full rationale and the
recorded deferral.

This plan is the single owner of `internal/access/policy/seed.go` for Phase 2.
Plans `02-08`, `02-09` and `02-10` consume these policies and prove their
behaviour; none of them edits this file, so no two plans race on it.

Purpose: seed policies are Go struct literals installed by `policy.Bootstrap` at
boot, so landing them together makes the whole family reviewable as one artifact —
which matters here, because §8.5.1's conjunction is only safe if the tier-floor
family and the row-keyed family stay in **separate evaluations**, and that property
is visible only when both are read side by side.
Output: ten new entries in `SeedPolicies()` and the `viewerProvider` test double
that lets later plans assert them.

**Open Question 1 settled here — and re-settled by D-29.** RESEARCH asked whether
`seed:profile-public-read` is one policy or two. PROFILE-11's scope covers **both**
public `entity_properties` rows and `characters.description`, which are two different
ABAC resources (`property:` and `character:`) gated today by two different
colocation-conditioned seeds. The single-policy alternative would need a bare
`resource` clause, which also matches `location:`, `object:` and `stream:`; RESEARCH
names that the most dangerous of the three options and this plan rejects it
explicitly.

The two-entry answer stands as the *shape*, but only the `property:` half **ships in
Phase 2**. D-29 defers `seed:profile-public-read-character` to Phase 4 because the
`character:` half cannot be widened safely until `characterToProto` stops returning
`PlayerId` and `LocationId` — and unlike the property half, `characters` has no
`visibility` column, so D-11's "change the row's visibility" remedy does not exist
for it. So Phase 2 ships **one** entry, `seed:profile-public-read-property`, and
records the second as deferred. The deviation from PROFILE-11's "one new seed policy"
wording is a naming choice, not a scope change; the deferral of the character half IS
a scope change and is recorded as such here, in Task 3's action, and in
`02-CONTEXT.md` D-29. Both are routed to `abac-reviewer` with D-05's §8.5.1.1
amendment.

**D-02 is implemented, not relitigated.** §8.5.1.1's option 2 — term B evaluating
against a co-located character subject and DENYING where one does not exist — is
REJECTED. An anonymous viewer has no character, `profile.pronouns` is an
`entity_properties` row seeded at the `anonymous` floor, so term B would deny it and
a reachable profile would carry `name` without `pronouns` — a §8.8 / INV-PRIVACY-10
violation that fails **closed** and therefore no test in the suite would catch. Term
B is issued against viewer-flavored read policies only.

**The viewer identity model is settled, not improvised (cross-AI review's sharpest
finding, 02-REVIEWS.md 02-07 HIGH).** The earlier draft of Task 3 wrote the twins
against `principal.viewer.player_id`. That cannot work and the review is right: the
shipped row semantics are **character**-keyed —
`resource.property.owner == principal.character.id` (`seed.go:120`) and
`principal.character.id in resource.property.visible_to` / `excluded_from`
(`:136`, `:142`) — so a player id compared against them never matches. And the
admin twin needs a `roles` attribute the viewer namespace did not have at all. A
missing or non-matching key is **false**
(`internal/access/policy/dsl/evaluator.go:216`), so `private`, `restricted` and
`admin` profile fields would have become permanently invisible with no error and no
failing test. The symptom — "the profile looks bare" — is exactly the one §8.5.1.1
records as provoking the forbidden repair of dropping term B.

The settlement, implemented in plan `02-13` and consumed here:

- **Ownership.** `PropertyProvider` derives `owner_player_id` from the row's own
  character-keyed `owner`, and the twin compares player to player.
- **`visible_to` / `excluded_from` for a player with multiple characters.**
  `PropertyProvider` derives `visible_to_players` and `excluded_from_players`, each
  in the direction that **cannot widen its policy's effect** — `02-CONTEXT.md`
  D-27. A player is in `visible_to_players` only when **every** character of theirs
  appears in `visible_to`; a player is in `excluded_from_players` when **any** of
  their characters appears in `excluded_from`. The plain union was proposed for
  both sides and declined for the permit side, because it broadens a grant made to
  a named character into a grant to the human behind it. Plan `02-13` carries a
  blocking checkpoint on that choice, so if the maintainer takes the union instead,
  these twins' semantics change without their DSL text changing — which is exactly
  why the direction is recorded in D-27 and in §8.5 rather than only in a provider.
- **Admin.** `ViewerTierProvider` emits `roles`, resolved per player through the
  same lookup `PlayerHasRole` semantics define, so the web and the operator socket
  cannot disagree about the same human.

Why derived peers rather than a viewer character-id list: the DSL cannot intersect
two attribute lists. `X in Y` needs a scalar left operand, and `containsAny` takes
literal needles only. Resolving the character→player relation once, server-side, in
the provider — where `ParentLocationResolver` already sets the precedent — is what
lets the twins evaluate the **same** row semantics D-01 requires instead of a
weaker approximation. Nothing here compares a player id to a character id.
