<objective>
Close the phase's row-level identity model: give the ABAC stack the attributes a
`viewer:` subject needs in order to satisfy the **same** `visibility` /
`visible_to` / `excluded_from` row semantics the shipped character-flavored
`seed:property-*` family evaluates, and wire every provider into the production
stack.

Purpose — **this plan exists because of cross-AI review HIGH #10/#11
(02-REVIEWS.md), which is the sharpest finding in the set.** The earlier plan had
the viewer twins comparing `principal.viewer.player_id` against row data that is
**character**-keyed. `seed:property-private-read` compares
`resource.property.owner == principal.character.id`
(`internal/access/policy/seed.go:120`), and `visible_to`/`excluded_from` are lists
of character ids (`internal/access/policy/seed.go:136,142`). A player id compared
against a character id never matches. Because a missing or non-matching key
evaluates **false** (`internal/access/policy/dsl/evaluator.go:216`), every
`private`, `restricted` and `admin` profile field would become permanently
invisible — no error, no failing test, fail-closed. The visible symptom is "the
profile looks bare", which §8.5.1.1 records as exactly the symptom that provokes
the forbidden repair of dropping term B.

**Why derived player-keyed peers, and not a viewer character-id list.** The
obvious fix — put the viewer's character ids in the subject bag — is only half
expressible in the shipped DSL, and the half it cannot express is the half that
matters. `X in Y` (`evalInExpr`) requires a **scalar** left operand and a slice
right operand, so `resource.property.owner in principal.viewer.character_ids`
would work for ownership; but `visible_to`/`excluded_from` need
"any of my characters is in this list", which is a list-to-list intersection.
`containsAll`/`containsAny` take a **literal** needle list
(`ContainsCondition.List`), not an attribute reference, so they cannot express it
either. There is no DSL expression that intersects two attribute lists. The
alternatives were: change the DSL grammar of a security-critical evaluator, or
resolve the character→player relation **server-side, in the provider, where every
other cross-entity resolution in this file already happens**. This plan takes the
second, following `ParentLocationResolver`
(`internal/access/policy/attribute/property.go:17-21,29-34`) as the precedent.

That is **not** the "substitute player_id for character IDs" the review warns
against. Nothing compares a player id to a character id. The provider resolves the
row's character-keyed fields into player-keyed peers once, server-side, and the
policy compares player to player.

Output: `PlayerRoles` behind a func-field seam, three derived `property.*` peers,
a character-owner resolver, and every provider registered in `BuildABACStack`.

**Derivation direction, stated as a decision (D-27) and gated (Task 0).** Each peer
is derived in the direction that **cannot widen its policy's effect**:

- **Permit-side — the ALL direction.** A player appears in `owner_player_id` or
  `visible_to_players` only when **every** character of that player appears in the
  row's corresponding character-keyed field.
- **Forbid-side — the ANY direction.** A player appears in `excluded_from_players`
  when **any** of that player's characters appears in `excluded_from`.

**What was proposed and deliberately not taken.** This plan originally specified a
plain union in both directions — "a player is a member iff ANY of their characters
is" — by analogy with `PlayerHasRole`'s any-character semantics. The analogy does
not carry. `PlayerHasRole` answers a question about a *player's* privileges, and
§10.5 makes that reading normative; `visible_to` and `owner` are row-level grants an
author made to *named characters*, and every shipped `seed:property-*` policy
compares them to `principal.character.id`
(`internal/access/policy/seed.go:117-143`). A union on the permit side therefore
broadens read access across a player's alternate characters — an authorization
widening no artifact had decided, proposed inside the phase whose subject is
privacy. Union remains correct for `excluded_from`, where it only ever denies more,
and it is kept there.

**The consequence, recorded rather than discovered.** A player holding two or more
characters does not receive an `owner` or `visible_to` permit through the web viewer
path unless the row names all of their characters, so the viewer path can be
**narrower** than the grid for identity-keyed rows. That is fail-closed and
deliberate; the cost is real and is exactly what Task 0's checkpoint exists to put
in front of a human before any of it is written.
</objective>
